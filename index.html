About my research

I work at the intersection of environmental data, spatial analysis, and artificial intelligence to investigate coastal rewilding potential across Europe. My research focuses particularly on three coastal habitats: intertidal mudflats, salt marshes, and seagrass meadows.

I study why coastal ecosystems degrade and how they may recover, reorganise and regain ecological functions and how they will survive the future climate extremes.

Research motivation

Coastal ecosystems play important roles in climate regulation, biodiversity conservation, shoreline protection and human well-being. Yet many of these ecosystems are under increasing pressure from climate change, biodiversity loss, land-use change and long histories of human intervention.

Coastal rewilding is an emerging field and its definition, the success indicators and resilience are still evolving.

My goal is to help make coastal rewilding more measurable and operational by developing quantitative frameworks and models that can be used to assess rewilding potential and ecological change.

By the end of my PhD, I aim to contribute decision-support tools, including a European coastal rewilding map and an analytical framework designed to be transparent, reusable and extensible by other researchers and practitioners.

Open Science & Data Practices

I am also interested in Open Science and research data management, particularly reproducible workflows, good documentation, data interoperability both by human and machine.

As a former Data Steward, I try to make my research as FAIR as possible.
